## Synopsis
The author supposes that when people introspect they (most times) try to find a singular truth that is the solution to all of their problems. This most times leads to a self defeating philosophy (x happened, so y cant happen) as opposed to letting a multitude of possible contributers naturally come. This need to have an axiom of our lives can lead us trapped into cyclicly thinking about our problems in what the author calls rumination. The author supposes that introspection is done correctly when used as a brainstorming activity and proposes that What questions over Why questions can lead us in this activity. (What caused x to happen over why did x happen (Why can be interpreted as asking for a singular truth)). The author supposes that rumination is so self-defeating that when this occurs we should have methods to stop it. The suggested ones are asking if anyone else cares (for mistakes made), asking what can be learned from a situation, and taking a pause either physically or mentally until the emotions around the situation have subsided.

## My feelings
The las part about rumination-busters as the author calls it I think is particularly effective when Mike firebombs my mornings. I need to mentally pause give time for each question rather than being as helpful as possible and awnsering them as they come to keep a level head. Or perhaps moving to a different location when I sense that the question barrage is coming. The ruminating on a single axiom is not something I feel philisophically attached to so I dont think I directly do this but I acknoweldge that when I am thinking about a situation I will often stop at what I feel is the first awnser rather than trying to chase a lead down untill all possible scenarios have been identified.

## Going Foreward
I will try starting to look at things with a what perspective over a why perspective to leave enough wiggle room for multiple causes.
